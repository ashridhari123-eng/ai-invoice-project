Orientation

What is Procure-to-Pay?

Procure-to-Pay (P2P) is the end-to-end business process that starts when someone in the company needs something and ends when the supplier is paid for delivering it.

Procure

Deciding what to buy, from whom, at what price, with whose approval. Owned mostly by the purchasing / procurement team.

Receive

Confirming the goods or services actually arrived, in the right quantity and quality. Owned by stores / operations.

Pay

Verifying the supplier's invoice against what was ordered and received, then releasing payment. Owned by accounts payable (AP) in finance.

Key mental model: P2P is a chain of documents. Each document authorises the next one. Your app's job is to create, route, match, and audit these documents.

Why it matters

Why companies invest in a P2P system

CONTROL

Nobody can spend company money without an approved document trail. No "surprise invoices" at month end. Every rupee is authorised before it's committed.

SAVINGS

Competitive quotes (RFQs) and negotiated rates typically save 3–10% on spend. Matching catches over-billing, duplicate invoices, and wrong prices.

COMPLIANCE

GST input credit needs valid invoices. TDS must be deducted correctly. MSME vendors must be paid within statutory timelines. Auditors want a clean trail.

SPEED

"Touchless" processing — invoices that flow from email to payment with zero human touches — cuts cycle time from weeks to days and frees the AP team for exceptions.

Look back at the reference dashboard: Touchless rate, Pending approvals, Avg cycle time, MSME at risk, Budget utilization, Sourcing savings — every KPI card is one of these four goals, measured.

The backbone

The P2P lifecycle — nine steps

01 · NEED

An employee or department needs goods/services — laptops, raw material, a marketing agency.

02 · PR

Purchase Requisition — an internal request: "please buy this". Not yet a commitment to any vendor.

03 · APPROVAL

The PR is routed through an approval matrix based on amount, category, and department budget.

04 · RFQ

Request for Quotation — sent to shortlisted vendors; their quotes are compared to pick a winner.

05 · PO

Purchase Order — a legally binding order to the chosen vendor: items, quantities, prices, terms.

06 · GRN

Goods Receipt Note — stores confirms what physically arrived (or a service entry sheet for services).

07 · INVOICE

The vendor sends their invoice — usually as a PDF over email. It must be captured and digitised.

08 · MATCH

2-way / 3-way matching — invoice vs PO vs GRN. Match → approve. Mismatch → exception queue.

09 · PAY

Payment run per due dates and terms; TDS deducted; posted to the ERP; vendor remittance advice sent.

Step 02 · PR

Purchase Requisition

The PR is an internal document. It never goes to a vendor. It answers: who wants what, why, by when, and against which budget.

A PR typically contains

  • Requester + department + cost centre
  • Line items: item/description, quantity, unit, estimated price
  • Need-by date and delivery location
  • Budget line it will consume
  • Justification / attachments (specs, screenshots)

Design implications for your app

  • PR has a status lifecycle: Draft → Submitted → Approved / Rejected / Returned
  • Approved PR commits budget (soft-blocks the amount)
  • One PR can split into multiple RFQs or POs; keep line-level links
  • Catalog items vs free-text items — catalog reduces junk data
Step 03

Approvals & the Delegation of Authority

Every company has a DOA (Delegation of Authority) — a policy saying who may approve how much. Your workflow engine encodes it.

Amount (₹)Approver chain (example)Notes
Up to 25,000Department managerSingle-step, auto-remind after 24h
25,001 – 2,00,000Manager → Department headSequential approval
2,00,001 – 10,00,000Manager → Dept head → Finance controllerFinance checks budget availability
Above 10,00,000… → CFO / DirectorMay require committee note

Concepts you must model

  • Sequential vs parallel approvals
  • Delegation (approver on leave → substitute)
  • Escalation on SLA breach
  • Auto-approval below a threshold (drives touchless rate)

Golden rule

The person who requests can never be the person who approves, and the person who approves the PO should not be the one who confirms receipt. This is called segregation of duties (SoD) — auditors will test your app for it.

Step 04 · RFQ

Sourcing: RFI vs RFQ vs RFP

DocumentQuestion it asks vendorsWhen used
RFI — Request for Information"What can you do? Tell us about yourselves."Discovering the market; new category
RFQ — Request for Quotation"Here's exactly what we need — what's your price?"Well-defined goods/services; price is the main variable
RFP — Request for Proposal"Here's our problem — propose a solution and price."Complex services, solutions with many approaches

Your app (and its AI comparison module in Module 3) focuses on the RFQ: structured line items sent to 3+ vendors, quotes received, compared side-by-side, one vendor awarded.

RFQ contents

  • Line items with specs, qty, unit
  • Delivery location & required date
  • Quote submission deadline
  • Commercial terms asked for: payment terms, freight, warranty, validity

Quote comparison basics

  • Never compare bare unit prices — compute landed cost (price + tax + freight + other charges)
  • Score non-price factors: delivery time, payment terms, vendor rating
  • Record the award decision & reason — that's your audit trail and your "Sourcing savings" KPI
Step 05 · PO

Purchase Order — the legal commitment

The PO is the first document a vendor sees. Once the vendor accepts it, it's a contract. Everything downstream — receipt, invoice, payment — is checked against the PO.

PO anatomy

  • Header: PO number, date, vendor, bill-to & ship-to entity, currency, payment terms (e.g. Net 30), incoterms if imported
  • Lines: item, HSN/SAC code, qty, unit price, tax rate (GST), discounts, delivery date
  • Totals: subtotal, CGST/SGST/IGST, freight, grand total
  • Terms & conditions attachment

Lifecycle & rules

  • Draft → Approved → Sent → Acknowledged → Partially received → Closed / Cancelled
  • Amendments create a new version — never silently edit an issued PO
  • Open PO qty = ordered − received; this drives 3-way matching
  • An approved PO converts the PR's budget commitment into a firmer obligation
Step 06 · GRN

Goods Receipt — proof it arrived

GRN (goods)

  • Created by stores against a PO when material arrives
  • Records received qty, accepted qty, rejected qty per line
  • Quality check may sit between receipt and acceptance
  • Partial deliveries → multiple GRNs against one PO

Service Entry Sheet (services)

  • For services there is no physical goods receipt
  • The project owner confirms "work done" — e.g. 40 hours of consulting delivered, milestone 2 completed
  • This confirmation plays the same role as a GRN in matching

Why receipts matter: without a GRN, you'd be paying based only on what the vendor claims they delivered. The GRN is the company's independent evidence — the third leg of the 3-way match.

Step 07 · INV

The vendor invoice (India specifics)

Must contain (GST-valid invoice)

  • Invoice number & date, vendor GSTIN, buyer GSTIN
  • Line items with HSN/SAC, qty, rate, taxable value
  • CGST + SGST (intra-state) or IGST (inter-state)
  • For vendors above the e-invoicing turnover threshold: IRN + QR code from the government portal

Realities your app must handle

  • Arrives as PDF over email (sometimes scanned images) — this is what your AI extraction module digitises
  • May not reference your PO number, or reference it wrongly
  • Credit notes / debit notes adjust earlier invoices
  • Non-PO invoices exist (utilities, rent) — they follow a coding + approval route instead of matching

TDS: for many service payments the buyer must deduct tax at source (e.g. 2% or 10% under various sections) and pay the vendor the net amount. Your payment module needs a TDS field per vendor/nature of service.

Step 08 · the heart of AP

2-way vs 3-way matching

Match typeDocuments comparedChecksUsed for
2-wayInvoice ↔ POPrice, qty billed ≤ qty ordered, totals, taxServices, subscriptions, low-risk categories
3-wayInvoice ↔ PO ↔ GRNAll of 2-way + qty billed ≤ qty received & acceptedPhysical goods — the default for material purchases
4-way+ Quality inspectionQty billed ≤ qty that passed QCRegulated / high-value manufacturing

Worked example

PO: 100 units @ ₹50. GRN: 90 received, 88 accepted. Invoice: 90 units @ ₹52.

→ Qty exception (billed 90 > accepted 88) and price exception (₹52 > ₹50). Both go to the exception queue with the buyer copied.

Tolerances

Real systems allow small deviations to avoid blocking everything: e.g. price within ±2% or ₹100, qty within ±1%. Within tolerance → auto-pass (counts toward the touchless rate). Outside → human review. Tolerances are configurable per category.

Step 09 · PAID

Payments

Concepts

  • Payment terms: Net 30/45/60, or "2/10 Net 30" (2% discount if paid in 10 days)
  • Due date = invoice date (or receipt date, per policy) + terms
  • Payment run: batch of approved invoices due, exported to the bank (or paid via ERP)
  • Advance payments: paid before delivery, adjusted against future invoices — see "Advances" in the reference app's sidebar

India-specific

  • TDS deducted at payment/credit, deposited monthly, Form 26Q filed quarterly
  • MSMED Act: registered micro & small enterprises must be paid within 45 days (or the agreed period if shorter) — else interest is payable and the expense can be disallowed for tax
  • That's exactly what the dashboard's "MSME at risk" card counts: payments nearing the 45-day limit
Money guardrails

Budgets & commitment accounting

A budget is an annual/quarterly spending limit per department + category. The clever part is tracking money in three states, not one:

COMMITTED

Approved PRs and open POs. Money you've promised but not yet been billed for.

ACCRUED

Goods received / invoices approved, not yet paid. A real liability.

SPENT

Payments actually made.

Budget check at PR approval: available = budget − committed − accrued − spent. If a new PR exceeds available budget, warn or block. The dashboard's "Budget utilization: committed vs allocated" card is this calculation.

Master data

Vendor master — the foundation everything rests on

Vendor record fields

  • Legal name, PAN, GSTIN (validate format & status), MSME/Udyam registration number & category
  • Bank account + IFSC (verify via penny-drop before first payment)
  • Payment terms, TDS section & rate, currency
  • Categories supplied, contacts, addresses
  • Status: Draft → Under review → Active → Blocked

Why it's critical

  • Fraud vector #1 is changing a vendor's bank account — bank-detail changes need maker-checker approval and an audit log entry
  • Wrong GSTIN → lost input tax credit
  • Missing MSME flag → statutory 45-day breach without anyone noticing
  • Duplicate vendors → duplicate payments
